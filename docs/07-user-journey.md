# User Journey

**Project:** LearnCS
**Document:** User Journey
**Phase:** 0, Project Discovery
**Status:** Draft: Pending Founder Approval

---

## Purpose

This document maps the journey of each primary persona through six stages of their learning lifecycle. It describes what users experience today, their goals, actions, pain points, and emotional states, without proposing solutions. Opportunities are framed as unmet needs, not as features.

All references trace back to Problem Statement (02), Target Users (03), Jobs To Be Done (04), Product Principles (05), and User Personas (06).

Primary personas include all Critical and High priority personas: Rami, Sarah, Liam, Aisha, and Carlos. Mei's journey (Medium priority) is included in condensed form for completeness.

# Journey 1: Rami, Self-Directed Learner

---

## Stage 1: Awareness

### Goals
Recognize that his current approach to learning CS is not working and that the problem is structural, not personal.

### Actions
- Notices he has been studying for months but still feels uncertain about his abilities.
- Reads posts on Reddit and dev communities from people with similar frustrations.
- Begins searching for "better ways to learn CS" and "how to know if I'm learning."

### Pain Points
- He blames himself for lack of progress rather than recognizing structural failures in available resources. (P3)
- The volume of advice is overwhelming and contradictory. (P5, P7)

### Questions
- "Am I just not smart enough for CS, or am I studying the wrong way?"
- "How do other self-taught developers actually succeed?"

### Emotional State
Frustrated. Doubting his own ability. Motivated but eroding.

### Related JTBD
E2 (Reduce Overwhelm), E3 (Know I'm Improving)

### Related Problems
P3, P5, P7

### Opportunities
The learner needs a way to understand that their struggle is caused by a broken system, not by personal deficiency. No current resource provides this reframing with evidence.

---

## Stage 2: Evaluation

### Goals
Find a learning approach or tool that addresses his specific frustrations, not just more content.

### Actions
- Compares platforms: freeCodeCamp, Coursera, Udemy, YouTube channels, The Odin Project.
- Reads reviews, Reddit recommendations, and "what I used to learn coding" articles.
- Tries free tiers of several platforms.
- Looks for something that can tell him what he already knows and what he should learn next.

### Pain Points
- Every platform looks the same from the outside: a catalog of courses or a linear track. He cannot determine which one, if any, addresses his core problem. (P7)
- Free trials and onboarding experiences do not assess his current knowledge, they ask him to self-report as "beginner/intermediate/advanced," which he cannot answer accurately. (P2)

### Questions
- "Which platform actually adapts to what I already know?"
- "How do I evaluate a learning tool if I can't evaluate my own knowledge?"

### Emotional State
Cautiously hopeful but skeptical. He has tried things before and been disappointed.

### Related JTBD
F1 (Assess Current Knowledge), E2 (Reduce Overwhelm)

### Related Problems
P2, P5, P7

### Opportunities
The learner needs a way to be accurately assessed during evaluation, before committing. No current platform demonstrates its value by showing the learner something true about their knowledge before asking for commitment.

---

## Stage 3: Learning

### Goals
Study CS concepts with confidence that he is working on the right material in the right order.

### Actions
- Follows whichever curriculum or resource he selected.
- Watches videos, reads explanations, writes code, solves exercises.
- Supplements with additional resources when the primary one is unclear.
- Attempts to build small projects to apply what he learns.

### Pain Points
- The curriculum is linear and not adapted to his state. He covers material he already knows and hits material he is not ready for. (P1)
- He finishes a lesson and is unsure whether he understood it. The only feedback is "correct/incorrect" on exercises. (P3, P4)
- When he gets stuck, he cannot identify whether the problem is with the current concept or with a gap in a prerequisite concept. (P2)
- He studies in isolation with no external feedback on his understanding. (P3)

### Questions
- "Did I actually understand that, or did I just follow along?"
- "I'm stuck, is this topic too hard, or am I missing something from earlier?"
- "Am I learning the right things for my goals?"

### Emotional State
Fluctuating between engagement and frustration. Good sessions feel productive. Bad sessions feel pointless. He cannot tell which is which.

### Related JTBD
F3 (What Next), F4 (Validate Understanding), F2 (Identify Gaps)

### Related Problems
P1, P2, P3, P4

### Opportunities
The learner needs real-time diagnostic feedback that identifies the root cause of confusion, prerequisite gap or current-concept difficulty. No current resource provides this.

---

## Stage 4: Progress

### Goals
See honest evidence that his CS understanding is growing over time.

### Actions
- Checks completion metrics on his current platform.
- Counts courses finished, exercises solved, projects built.
- Occasionally re-attempts old exercises to see if they feel easier.
- Compares himself to other learners in communities.

### Pain Points
- Completion metrics do not reflect understanding. He has "completed" courses where he still has gaps. (P2, P3)
- He has no way to visualize his knowledge, which topics are strong, which are weak, which are missing entirely.
- Old exercises feel easier, but he cannot distinguish between genuine understanding and familiarity with the exercise. (P4)
- Comparing to others is demoralizing and unreliable, everyone is on a different path. (P5)

### Questions
- "How much do I actually know compared to a month ago?"
- "Am I on track, or am I falling behind where I should be?"
- "Which areas have I actually mastered and which just feel familiar?"

### Emotional State
Anxious. Progress feels invisible. Effort feels potentially wasted. Motivation requires willpower rather than evidence.

### Related JTBD
F6 (Track Real Progress), E3 (Know I'm Improving)

### Related Problems
P2, P3, P4

### Opportunities
The learner needs a truthful, knowledge-based progress visualization, not consumption metrics. No current system tracks understanding growth over time at the concept level.

---

## Stage 5: Mastery

### Goals
Achieve genuine competence in core CS topics, deep enough to apply them confidently and independently.

### Actions
- Attempts increasingly complex projects and problems.
- Tries to solve problems without reference material.
- Begins contributing to open-source or building original projects.
- Revisits topics he studied earlier to test retention.

### Pain Points
- He has no clear definition of what "mastery" means for any given topic. There is no threshold or standard to aim for. (P4)
- He discovers decay, topics he studied months ago are no longer solid. No system warned him or suggested review. (P2)
- He cannot distinguish between "I've mastered this" and "I've practiced this specific type of problem many times." (P4)

### Questions
- "How do I know when I've truly mastered a concept?"
- "Which of my older topics have decayed and need review?"
- "Is my understanding deep enough to apply to novel situations?"

### Emotional State
Mixed. Growing confidence in some areas. Persistent doubt in others. Aware that mastery is non-linear but lacking tools to manage it.

### Related JTBD
F4 (Validate Understanding), F6 (Track Real Progress), E4 (Evidence-Based Confidence)

### Related Problems
P2, P4

### Opportunities
The learner needs mastery thresholds defined externally and knowledge decay tracking that triggers review proactively. Neither capability exists in any current platform.

---

## Stage 6: Career Transition

### Goals
Convert his CS knowledge into professional employment.

### Actions
- Builds a portfolio of projects.
- Applies for junior developer positions.
- Prepares for technical interviews by grinding practice problems.
- Attempts to demonstrate competence through GitHub, personal website, and cover letters.

### Pain Points
- He has no credible credential. His self-education carries no verifiable weight with employers. (P8)
- Interview preparation feels disconnected from his learning. He grinds LeetCode problems in a mode that feels different from his actual understanding. (P4, P6)
- He does not know which roles he is qualified for. Job postings list requirements he cannot evaluate against his own knowledge. (P5)
- He cannot articulate his specific competencies to employers in a structured, trustworthy way. (P8)

### Questions
- "Am I ready to apply?"
- "How do I prove what I know without a degree?"
- "Which jobs match my actual knowledge level?"

### Emotional State
High anxiety. Imposter syndrome at peak intensity. The transition from learner to professional feels like a cliff rather than a gradient.

### Related JTBD
F9 (Role Readiness), S1 (Demonstrate to Employers), E4 (Evidence-Based Confidence)

### Related Problems
P5, P8

### Opportunities
The learner needs a way to map his verified knowledge against role requirements and to present that mapping in a form employers find credible. No current system connects learning state to hiring requirements.

# Journey 2: Sarah, Career Switcher

---

## Stage 1: Awareness

### Goals
Recognize that a career in software engineering is viable for her and understand the scope of what she needs to learn.

### Actions
- Reads articles about career switching into tech.
- Evaluates bootcamps, online degrees, and self-study paths.
- Calculates her financial runway and time constraints.
- Talks to people in tech to understand the landscape.

### Pain Points
- The information landscape is overwhelming. Bootcamp marketing, influencer advice, and community opinions conflict. (P5, P7)
- She cannot assess the realistic scope of what she needs to learn. Estimates range from "3 months" to "4 years." (P5)

### Questions
- "Is this realistic for me?"
- "How long will this actually take?"
- "Should I do a bootcamp, a degree, or self-study?"

### Emotional State
Excited but anxious. The decision to switch is high-stakes and partially irreversible.

### Related JTBD
E2 (Reduce Overwhelm), E1 (Path Confidence)

### Related Problems
P5, P7

### Opportunities
The learner needs a realistic, personalized assessment of scope, not marketing claims or anecdotal success stories. No current resource provides honest, individualized estimates.

---

## Stage 2: Evaluation

### Goals
Select the learning path that is most likely to produce employment within her financial runway.

### Actions
- Compares bootcamps by cost, duration, job placement rates, and reviews.
- Evaluates free and paid online resources.
- Attempts to create a self-study plan based on roadmaps and community advice.
- Tries introductory modules on several platforms.

### Pain Points
- She cannot evaluate options because she lacks the domain knowledge to assess what's important. (P5)
- Bootcamp marketing is optimistic. Community opinions are anecdotal. She has no objective data. (P8)
- No platform assesses her existing skills (SQL, basic Python, analytical thinking) and factors them into a path. (P2)

### Questions
- "Which option gives me the best chance of getting hired?"
- "Can my existing analytical skills accelerate my learning?"
- "Which bootcamp/course/resource is actually good, not just well-marketed?"

### Emotional State
Decision fatigue. Pressure to choose correctly because the cost of switching later is high.

### Related JTBD
F1 (Assess Current Knowledge), F5 (Personalized Path)

### Related Problems
P2, P5

### Opportunities
The learner needs her existing skills assessed and factored into a recommendation. No current platform recognizes transferable skills from non-CS backgrounds.

---

## Stage 3: Learning

### Goals
Acquire CS knowledge and practical skills as efficiently as possible within her time constraints.

### Actions
- Follows her chosen path (bootcamp, course, or self-study plan).
- Studies 6-8 hours daily on a structured schedule she created.
- Practices coding through exercises and a portfolio project.
- Supplements with interview preparation materials.

### Pain Points
- Her path feels uncertain even while following it. She isn't sure the topics she's covering are the right ones for her goal. (P5)
- She memorizes patterns for coding problems but senses this isn't real understanding. (P4)
- She can't tell which concepts she actually understands vs. which she just encountered. (P3)
- Theory and practice feel disconnected. She studies algorithms in one session and builds React components in another, the connection is unclear. (P6)

### Questions
- "Am I spending my time on the right things?"
- "Am I actually understanding this, or just getting familiar with it?"
- "How does this algorithm concept connect to the software I'm building?"

### Emotional State
Determined but stressed. The financial clock amplifies every doubt.

### Related JTBD
F3 (What Next), F4 (Validate Understanding), F7 (Theory-Practice Connection)

### Related Problems
P1, P3, P4, P6

### Opportunities
The learner needs a path that dynamically adjusts based on validated understanding, and a way to see how each concept connects to her career goal. No current resource links learning content to career outcomes in real time.

---

## Stage 4: Progress

### Goals
Verify that she is on track to reach job-readiness within her runway.

### Actions
- Checks course completion metrics.
- Tracks exercises solved and projects built.
- Attempts mock interview problems as a readiness test.
- Compares her timeline to bootcamp graduates and other career switchers.

### Pain Points
- Her progress metrics measure activity, not readiness. She has no way to know if she's 40% ready or 80% ready. (P2, P3)
- Mock interview performance is inconsistent, she passes some and fails others, with no diagnostic explanation. (P4)
- Comparing to others is misleading, everyone started with different backgrounds. (P5)

### Questions
- "Am I on track?"
- "At this rate, will I be ready before my savings run out?"
- "What is the gap between where I am and where I need to be?"

### Emotional State
Anxious. Progress is invisible. The financial runway creates constant time pressure.

### Related JTBD
F6 (Track Real Progress), F9 (Role Readiness), E1 (Path Confidence)

### Related Problems
P2, P3, P5

### Opportunities
The learner needs readiness tracking calibrated against career requirements, not generic progress indicators. No current system measures distance-to-employment.

---

## Stage 5: Mastery

### Goals
Achieve the specific level of competence required for her target role, not comprehensive CS mastery, but targeted career-ready mastery.

### Actions
- Focuses intensively on interview preparation topics.
- Drills coding problems daily.
- Attempts to build more complex features in her portfolio projects.
- Reviews topics that feel weak.

### Pain Points
- She doesn't know which areas need mastery and which need only familiarity for her target role. (P5)
- She is unsure whether her "mastery" of interview topics reflects real understanding or pattern drilling. (P4)
- Knowledge decay is real, topics she studied two months ago are fading. (P2)

### Questions
- "Do I need to master everything, or just specific areas?"
- "Am I genuinely ready, or have I just drilled enough patterns to feel ready?"

### Emotional State
Urgency intensifying. Oscillating between cautious confidence and acute doubt.

### Related JTBD
F4 (Validate Understanding), F9 (Role Readiness), E4 (Evidence-Based Confidence)

### Related Problems
P2, P4, P5

### Opportunities
The learner needs role-specific mastery criteria that distinguish "you understand this" from "you've memorized this." No current system defines what "ready for X role" actually means at the concept level.

---

## Stage 6: Career Transition

### Goals
Get hired as a software engineer and successfully perform the job.

### Actions
- Applies to positions matching her target role.
- Attends technical interviews.
- Negotiates offers.
- Begins onboarding at a new company.

### Pain Points
- She has no CS degree and her credentials are weak. She must outperform the interview to compensate. (P8)
- Technical interviews test skills in a format different from how she learned them. (P4, P6)
- She cannot articulate her specific competencies beyond listing technologies she has used. (P8)
- She fears that even if she gets hired, she won't be able to perform the job, because she's not sure her understanding is genuine. (P4)

### Questions
- "Can I convince them I'm qualified without a CS degree?"
- "If I get the job, will I actually be able to do it?"
- "How do I communicate what I know when I don't have traditional credentials?"

### Emotional State
Peak anxiety. The stakes are highest here. Every interview outcome feels definitive.

### Related JTBD
S1 (Demonstrate to Employers), F9 (Role Readiness), E4 (Evidence-Based Confidence)

### Related Problems
P5, P6, P8

### Opportunities
The learner needs a credible, verifiable competency signal that employers can interpret without relying solely on degrees or interviews. This signal does not exist in the current ecosystem.

# Journey 3: Liam, University CS Student

---

## Stage 1: Awareness

### Goals
Realize that passing courses is not the same as understanding the material.

### Actions
- Notices difficulty applying coursework concepts to personal projects or interview preparation.
- Hears upperclassmen talk about being unprepared for technical interviews despite good grades.
- Begins to question whether his grades reflect genuine understanding.

### Pain Points
- His institution's assessment reinforces the illusion that passing equals understanding. (P4)
- No system or person proactively identifies the gap between his grades and his actual knowledge. (P2)

### Questions
- "Am I actually learning this, or just getting good at exams?"
- "Will I be ready for the real world when I graduate?"

### Emotional State
Nagging doubt beneath surface-level confidence. Not yet urgent enough to act.

### Related JTBD
E3 (Know I'm Improving), F4 (Validate Understanding)

### Related Problems
P2, P4

### Opportunities
The learner needs a feedback mechanism independent of institutional grades that assesses genuine understanding. No supplement to university education currently provides this.

---

## Stage 2: Evaluation

### Goals
Find supplementary resources that fill the gaps his university leaves.

### Actions
- Explores YouTube channels, MIT OpenCourseWare, and free platforms.
- Asks classmates and online communities for recommendations.
- Tries a few resources but has limited time alongside his coursework.

### Pain Points
- He cannot identify his specific gaps, so he doesn't know what to supplement. (P2)
- Supplementary resources are full courses, he needs targeted gap-filling, not another curriculum. (P1)

### Questions
- "Which of my courses left gaps?"
- "What should I study that my university doesn't cover?"

### Emotional State
Mildly concerned but not yet acting with urgency.

### Related JTBD
F2 (Identify Gaps), F1 (Assess Current Knowledge)

### Related Problems
P1, P2

### Opportunities
The learner needs a diagnostic that identifies concept-level gaps within the topics his courses supposedly covered. No current tool assesses intra-course understanding gaps.

---

## Stage 3: Learning

### Goals
Deepen understanding of course material beyond exam-passing level and fill foundational gaps.

### Actions
- Studies course material more carefully, going beyond what's required for exams.
- Watches supplementary video explanations.
- Attempts to connect theory to practical applications through side projects.

### Pain Points
- His curriculum's fixed pace doesn't allow him to slow down on difficult topics. (P1)
- Supplementary resources don't align with his course content, different terminology, different order, different emphasis. (P7)
- He studies theory but has no structured way to practice applying it. (P6)
- Feedback comes only from graded assignments, which test the wrong things. (P3, P4)

### Questions
- "Do I actually understand memory management, or did I just memorize the slides?"
- "How would I use what I learned in Operating Systems in a real project?"

### Emotional State
Intellectually engaged but unsupported. Wants to go deeper but lacks the tools and time.

### Related JTBD
F4 (Validate Understanding), F7 (Theory-Practice Connection)

### Related Problems
P1, P3, P4, P6

### Opportunities
The learner needs a way to validate understanding at the concept level, independent of university assessment. He also needs practical application contexts for theoretical material his courses don't provide.

---

## Stage 4: Progress

### Goals
Understand whether his actual CS competence is growing, independent of his GPA.

### Actions
- Tracks grades (institutional progress).
- Attempts interview-style problems to test practical readiness.
- Reflects on whether he "feels" stronger in the material.

### Pain Points
- Grades reflect exam performance, not understanding. (P4)
- Interview-style problems test a different set of skills than his courses develop. (P6)
- He has no knowledge map, no picture of which concepts he has genuinely mastered across all his courses. (P2)

### Questions
- "If I were interviewed today, how would I do?"
- "Which of my courses actually produced lasting understanding?"

### Emotional State
Growing unease as graduation approaches. Aware that grades may not translate to readiness.

### Related JTBD
F6 (Track Real Progress), F1 (Assess Current Knowledge), E3 (Know I'm Improving)

### Related Problems
P2, P4, P6

### Opportunities
The learner needs a competence assessment independent of institutional grades that shows his real knowledge map, strengths, weaknesses, and blind spots, across his entire education.

---

## Stage 5: Mastery

### Goals
Achieve deep understanding in core CS topics before graduating.

### Actions
- Focuses study on topics he suspects are weak.
- Takes on a substantial capstone or personal project.
- Begins serious interview preparation.

### Pain Points
- His suspicions about weak topics are unvalidated guesses. (P2)
- The capstone project reveals gaps he didn't know existed. (P2, P6)
- Interview preparation feels like learning a separate skill rather than applying what he studied. (P4, P6)

### Questions
- "Why does interview prep feel so different from what I studied in class?"
- "Are the gaps I'm finding now things I should have learned, or things my curriculum never covered?"

### Emotional State
Urgent concern. Graduation is approaching and he doesn't feel ready.

### Related JTBD
F2 (Identify Gaps), F7 (Theory-Practice Connection), F4 (Validate Understanding)

### Related Problems
P2, P4, P6

### Opportunities
The learner needs early, continuous identification of understanding gaps throughout his degree, not discovery at the end via capstone or interview failure.

---

## Stage 6: Career Transition

### Goals
Graduate and secure a software engineering position where he can perform from day one.

### Actions
- Applies for jobs. Attends career fairs. Prepares for interviews.
- Relies on his degree as his primary credential.
- Builds a small portfolio to supplement academic credentials.

### Pain Points
- His degree signals that he attended a program, not that he understands CS at a professional level. (P8)
- Technical interviews reveal gaps his grades hid. (P4, P8)
- He needs practical skills (Git, deployment, code review) that his curriculum didn't teach. (P6)

### Questions
- "Will my degree be enough?"
- "How do I demonstrate I'm better than my transcript suggests, or worse?"

### Emotional State
Anxiety about the gap between education and professional expectations. Hopes the degree carries enough weight.

### Related JTBD
S1 (Demonstrate to Employers), F9 (Role Readiness)

### Related Problems
P6, P8

### Opportunities
The learner needs a way to supplement his degree with granular competency evidence that addresses the gaps employers know degrees leave.

# Journey 4: Aisha, Bootcamp Graduate

---

## Stage 1: Awareness

### Goals
Understand that her interview failures are caused by specific, fillable gaps, not by fundamental inadequacy.

### Actions
- Reviews interview feedback (often vague: "not strong enough on fundamentals").
- Searches online for "CS fundamentals for bootcamp grads."
- Reads experiences of other bootcamp graduates facing similar problems.

### Pain Points
- Interview feedback is non-diagnostic. She knows she failed but not exactly why. (P3, P4)
- She conflates "I don't have a CS degree" with "I can't learn this." (P8)

### Questions
- "Is the problem that I went to a bootcamp instead of university?"
- "Can I fill these gaps without doing a full CS degree?"

### Emotional State
Discouraged but determined. Each rejection hurts but hasn't stopped her yet.

### Related JTBD
F2 (Identify Gaps), E4 (Evidence-Based Confidence)

### Related Problems
P3, P4, P8

### Opportunities
The learner needs diagnostic feedback from interview failures, not just "you failed," but "you failed because of X specific gap." No current system provides this.

---

## Stage 2: Evaluation

### Goals
Find a resource that teaches CS fundamentals without forcing her to repeat web development basics she already knows.

### Actions
- Evaluates CS courses (MIT OCW, Coursera, edX, YouTube series).
- Looks for "CS for bootcamp graduates" resources.
- Tries starting a few courses and checks whether they respect her existing knowledge.

### Pain Points
- Every CS course starts from scratch. Variables, loops, basic syntax, she knows all of this. (P1)
- No platform can identify which specific CS topics she already understands from her bootcamp and practical experience. (P2)
- She doesn't know which CS topics matter for her interviews and which don't. (P5)

### Questions
- "Which CS topics specifically do I need?"
- "Can anything assess my current level so I don't waste time?"

### Emotional State
Impatient. She needs targeted help, not another beginning-to-end curriculum.

### Related JTBD
F1 (Assess Current Knowledge), F8 (No Redundant Learning)

### Related Problems
P1, P2, P5

### Opportunities
The learner needs an assessment that identifies her exact CS knowledge boundary, what she already knows from practice and what she genuinely lacks. No current platform offers this for non-traditional learners.

---

### Goals
Fill the specific CS foundational gaps that are preventing her from succeeding in technical interviews.

### Actions

- Solves LeetCode problems targeting her weak areas.

### Pain Points
- She must self-diagnose which topics to study, which requires the very knowledge she lacks. (P2)
- She solves problems but can't tell if she understood the solution or memorized a pattern. (P3, P4)

Focused but uncertain. Every study session is shadowed by the question of whether it's working.

### Related Problems
P2, P3, P4, P6

### Opportunities
The learner needs validation of understanding that goes beyond problem-solving correctness, she needs to know her mental model is sound, not just her output.

## Stage 4: Progress

See whether her gap-filling effort is actually improving her interview readiness.

### Questions

P2, P3, P5

- "Is my understanding robust enough for a real interview, with time pressure and unexpected variations?"

Cautiously growing confidence, tempered by awareness that real mastery feels different from drilled familiarity.

### Related JTBD
F4 (Validate Understanding), E4 (Evidence-Based Confidence)

### Related Problems
P2, P4

### Opportunities
The learner needs a distinction between pattern-level fluency and concept-level understanding, with assessment that specifically probes the latter.

## Stage 6: Career Transition

Get hired and prove, to herself and her employer, that she can do the job.

### Questions

Relief mixed with residual imposter syndrome. Needs early on-the-job success to build real confidence.

### Related JTBD
S1 (Demonstrate to Employers), E4 (Evidence-Based Confidence)

### Related Problems
P4, P6, P8

### Opportunities
The learner needs a post-hiring competency signal, evidence that her knowledge is genuine and growing, that resolves imposter syndrome through data rather than reassurance.

# Journey 5: Carlos, Junior Developer

---

## Stage 1: Awareness

### Goals
Recognize that his on-the-job struggles are caused by specific knowledge gaps, not general incompetence.

### Actions
- Notices patterns in the problems he can't solve independently.
- Observes that senior developers reason about problems differently, not just faster.
- Realizes that his struggles are conceptual, not just experiential.

### Pain Points
